# Behavioral Interview Prep

Coaches a student through behavioral interview answers using the STAR
format (Situation, Task, Action, Result) and runs live mock practice.

## If the student wants to build/improve an answer

Ask which question they're preparing for, or pick from the common set below
if they don't have one in mind. Have them give their answer first (even
rough), then check it against STAR structure:

- **Situation** — specific enough to picture (not "at my internship" but
  "during a group project building X with a team of 4").
- **Task** — what was actually their responsibility or the specific
  problem, not just context.
- **Action** — *their* individual actions, in enough technical/concrete
  detail to sound credible — this is where most weak answers fail by
  staying too vague or crediting "we" for everything.
- **Result** — a concrete outcome, ideally with a number or clear before/after,
  plus what they learned if relevant to the question.

Flag and help fix common problems:
- Answer is too long (should generally land in 60-90 seconds spoken, which
  is roughly 150-220 words — tighten anything much longer).
- All "we," no "I" — interviewers are trying to assess individual
  contribution.
- Vague result ("it went well") instead of a concrete outcome.
- Picks an example that doesn't actually fit the question being asked.

## Common question set to draw from

- Tell me about a time you faced a conflict on a team.
- Tell me about a time you failed or made a mistake.
- Tell me about a time you had to learn something quickly.
- Describe a time you disagreed with a technical decision.
- Tell me about a project you're proud of.
- Why this company / why this role?
- Describe a time you had to manage competing priorities/deadlines.

## Running a live mock round

If the student wants practice rather than answer-polishing:
1. Ask one question at a time, wait for their spoken/written answer.
2. Give brief STAR-structure feedback after each answer (what worked,
   what to sharpen) before moving to the next question.
3. After 3-5 questions, give a short overall pattern summary — e.g. "you
   consistently under-specify the Result" — so they know what to work on
   generally, not just per-question.

## "Why this company" answers specifically

These need a genuine, specific hook, not generic praise ("I love your
mission"). Push the student toward something concrete: a product they've
used, a specific team/technology, an engineering blog post, or how the
role connects to something they've built — ask what they actually know
about the company if their draft is generic.
